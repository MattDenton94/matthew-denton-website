## What she needed

Nautical Aerial & Fitness is a pole and aerial studio — REPSA-certified, Acrobatic Arts-registered, IPSF-trained instruction, three years in. That's a genuinely serious credential list, and the old site didn't carry any of it. It needed to read as premium and considered, not like a hobby class listing, and it needed to speak to a wider audience than "flexible 20-somethings" — the studio also trains elderly and active-ageing clients, pre- and post-natal clients, and people doing rehab and mobility work.

## What I designed and built

A dark, editorial site built around real photography instead of stock imagery — a duotone hero image, an italic accent word doing the heavy lifting on the headline, and enough restraint elsewhere that the photography stays the focus. The "who this is for" list sits right up front on the About section, so a 55-year-old considering rehab training and a 25-year-old chasing an aerial silks routine both immediately see themselves in it.

The section rhythm breaks from dark to a warm off-white for Classes & Equipment — partly to let the cooler, teal-toned apparatus photography breathe, partly so the page doesn't read as one long dark scroll. Pricing stays direct: six cards, one clearly marked best value, no hidden tiers.

Every CTA routes to WhatsApp, because that's how this studio's clients actually book.

## The result

A site that looks like what it's selling — controlled, considered, a little bit performance-art. It's live, it's hers, and it's still exactly what a first-time visitor needs to decide to message.
